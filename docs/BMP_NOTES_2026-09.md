# The El Niño BMPs (email thread 3–4 Sep 2026) read against the site model

*Mo Sharif, 8 Sep 2026. Planning-level; every number below is the app's, from the January-2024
lidar, EA's CAD, the August-2026 Jacobs survey and the provisional Atlas 14 depths, and says so.*

Figure: `docs/bmp/bmp_sites_2026-09.jpg` — the four BMP sites on the Jan-2024 ortho with the
pipes, the grates, the surveyed sandbag wall and the impoundment's two rim lows.

## What the thread is asking for

EPA's concern is one El Niño winter (the remedy starts in 2027): no large flow carrying
contaminated material down the channel from the Herman Impoundment (HI) to Clear Lake, and no
visible plume in the lake. The proposed measures are "just in case", cheapest first:

1. **Reinforce the sandbag blockade on the two 24-in HI overflow pipes**, with plastic sheeting
   (a USACE / FEMA sandbag-dam detail, not bare bags).
2. **Straw bales across the pipeline valley** at the concrete transition structures. Not wattles
   and not a skid steer — the above-ground pipes lie on the channel floor and nothing gets past
   the first bend.
3. **The SBM Road inlet near Green Pond**: hay bales at the toe of the waste rock where it meets
   the road ditch and beside the grate, and the grate kept clear (a plugged grate ponds the road
   full width). This inlet discharges straight into HI.
4. **The WRD western face**: new wattles along the toe by the access road, and the small road
   ditches that turn clean water into the rock armour refreshed. Re-doing the wattles on the
   face itself is a week for three.

Kat wants these as line items in the sub SOW at daily / linear-foot rates so the crew can
respond without a new scope. Sefa's H&H model says HI does not overtop in the 100-yr or the
500-yr even starting full at WSE 1340.5, and the "adjacent area" at his point A makes 69.6 cfs
(100-yr) / 101.2 cfs (500-yr). Lucero's field note: the 30-in that conveys Green Pond is in
good condition, the dual 24-in are in disrepair.

## What the site model says about each

### The three pipes, and one correction to the app

The trench from the sandbag wall to the lake carries three pipes side by side (EA lines
E943E / E943C / E943D): the two 24-in HI overflow barrels (surveyed inverts 1341.53 S /
1341.57 N, 24-in corrugated HDPE) and, on the south, the pipe that takes Green Pond's overflow
and the SBM Road grates (Spots 8–15) to the same outfall. The app had that third pipe down as
a 24-in from the drafted spacing; **it is the 30-in**, per the thread, and the network now
says so. Nothing computes with that size yet — no inverts have been surveyed on it.

### 1. The sandbag dam: what it holds back, and what "covering the pipes" buys

| level | ft above the Aug-2026 water (1336.45) | storage above today |
|---|---|---|
| first discharge — the pipe inverts, 1341.55 | +5.10 | 109.2 ac-ft |
| sandbag crest, surveyed 1343.54 | +7.09 | 153.8 ac-ft |
| **lowest lidar rim ①, 1343.84** (west rim, E 6,371,927 N 2,127,693) | +7.39 | 160.7 ac-ft |
| rim ② by the wall, 1344.34 | +7.89 | — |

The impoundment holds about 22 ac-ft per foot of rise (20.6 ac of water at today's level).

Lucero: "covering the HI overflow pipes gives us almost 4 feet from the 24-inch inverts to the
top of the road". The road is ~4 ft above the inverts, but **the impoundment's lowest rim is
not at the road**: it is the west rim ①, 200 ft north of the pipes, at 1343.84 — 2.3 ft above
the inverts and only 0.3 ft above the surveyed sandbag crest. Water that could no longer leave
through the pipes would rise past the sandbag crest first and then leave over ①, and the app
traces that spill **down the same pipeline valley to the lake** (999 ft, ending 100 ft short of
the outfall). So blocking the pipes buys about 2.0 ft / 45 ac-ft to the sandbag crest and
2.3 ft / 51 ac-ft to the rim — not 4 ft — and the water still arrives in the valley the BMPs
are protecting, just over a rim instead of through a pipe. Lucero's groundwater point stands
on top of that. This is the strongest argument in the thread for *reinforcing* the dam rather
than *raising* it, and for the valley bales (item 2) either way.

### 2. The pipeline valley: everything meets here

Both HI rim lows and all three pipes discharge into this one valley. The valley's own overland
catchment is small — about 8 ac reaches the channel 200 ft above the lake — so what the bales
are really for is a pipe failure (the dual 24-in "in disrepair" running with HI water in
them), a HI spill over the rim, or a Green Pond overflow leaving the 30-in through a bad
joint. EA drew no structures along the trench other than the junction grate and the outfall,
so the "concrete transition structures" are not in the app; they belong in the field
walk-down with a GPS point each (the Position button in the app's field mode will do it).

### 3. The SBM Road inlet near Green Pond

Two candidates in the app, both on SBM Road and both draining toward HI, and the thread does
not say which:

- the **round inlet at Green Pond's NW corner** (E 6,373,917 N 2,127,966), which EA's culvert
  mark E5D2D takes 62 ft under the gravel road toward HI. The lidar drainage map gives it 1.3 ac.
- the **Spot 8 grate** 90 ft further west (E 6,373,831 N 2,127,919), which is the head of the
  road-drain chain to the 30-in — it goes to the lake, not to HI.

Either way the lidar says the same thing the thread says: the road ditch on the south side
carries the South WRP's runoff *past* the grates, overland, into HI — that ditch is part of
the 37.9 ac that reach the impoundment directly. A plugged grate ponds the road because the
ditch has nowhere else to go. Bales at the toe and at the grate are sediment control, not
flow control; the water goes to HI with or without them.

### 4. The WRD western face

The dam's west face is the slope between HI's west shore and the pipeline valley, with the
access road along its toe; rim low ① sits on its crest. Wattles along the toe keep the face's
sediment out of the road and the valley; the road-ditch refresh keeps clean road runoff in
the rock armour rather than down the road surface. Nothing to add from the model except that
this face is exactly where a rim spill would go, so the two measures back each other up.

## Sefa's model against the app's numbers — one question worth asking

Sefa: HI does not overtop from a full start at 1340.5, 100-yr or 500-yr, climate-adjusted,
"we get close". The app agrees in direction and it turns on one number:

- from **1340.5** there is ~1.05 ft / **23 ac-ft** of storage before the inverts;
- the 100-yr 24-h depth here is 8.3 in (provisional Atlas 14); at the composite CN of ~82
  that is about 6.1 in of runoff;
- the impoundment's **direct** catchment is **37.9 ac** → ~19 ac-ft, which is "close but no
  discharge" — Sefa's answer;
- its **whole** catchment is **84 ac** (the extra 46 ac reaches it through small depressions on
  the way; the flow-accumulation kernel puts it at 84.1 independently) → ~43 ac-ft, which is
  ~0.9 ft over the inverts and the pipes running.

The difference between the two is antecedent condition: in a dry single event the small
depressions take the first inch or two; in an El Niño winter they are already full and the
whole 84 ac contributes. **Worth asking Sefa which contributing area the model uses for HI
and whether the depressions are treated as full** — it is the difference between "close" and
"discharging" in exactly the scenario the BMPs are for. Either way, from *today's* 1336.45
even the 100-yr storm on 84 ac (43 ac-ft) is 40 % of the 109 ac-ft to the inverts: it takes a
wet season, not a storm, to get there — which is what the 1340.5 "full" starting WSE in the
Boer TM represents.

The **69.6 cfs "adjacent area"** at point A cannot be reproduced from the app: the valley's own
overland runoff is ~8 ac, far too small, and the 30-in's contributing area (Frog Pond, Green
Pond and the road, 198 ac) is far too big unless the two ponds are routed. If point A is the
outfall, 70 cfs reads like the routed Frog/Green outflow plus the valley; if it is the 30-in's
capacity, that is a different number. One line from Sefa settles it.
