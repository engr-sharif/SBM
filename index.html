SBMM Site Explorer

The daily digital twin for the residential remedy and Phase 1 construction. Everything is organised by what you are doing — navigating, inspecting, measuring, designing, reviewing drawings — not by which file the data came from.

One layer state. A layer that is on is on in 2D, in 3D, in the sheet windows and in the exports. The 3D view has no visibility checkboxes of its own: what it draws is whatever the Layers tree says is showing. Layer choices are remembered between sessions and travel in the session file — except the cultural group, which is off at every start by design.

Tools are modes. Exactly one is active at a time, the pill at the bottom of the stage says which and what it is waiting for next, and Esc always returns to Navigate — it cancels whatever is half-drawn, drops the tool and clears the button. Hold Space to pan without leaving the mode you are in.

Grabbing a vertex handle enters edit and releasing it returns you where you were. While sketching: click vertices, Enter or double-click finishes, right-click or Backspace removes the last vertex, CtrlZ undoes.
Undo and redo cover every action — drawing, vertex edits, modify commands, pads, sections, smart boundaries, raindrops and deletes. CtrlZ or UNDO steps back, CtrlY (or CtrlShiftZ, or REDO) steps forward again, 100 steps each way. A new action after an undo drops the branch you left, the way every editor does.

Layout — the left dock has Layers, My work (every drawing you have made: rename, show/hide, lock, zoom to, organise into folders) and Sheets (a card per drawing with a thumbnail, filtered by lot). The right dock has the Inspector (the selected object’s properties and edits) and Results (measurement and volume cards, copy as CSV). Selecting anything brings the Inspector forward; running a computation brings Results forward. Both docks collapse to their icon rail and drag wider. The status bar shows E, N, Z, lat/long, scale and snap state in both views.

Layers — six groups, in the order the work happens in:
  Base — orthoimagery, hillshade, lidar contours, canopy, slope, aspect.
  Site framework — decision units, waste piles, parcels, buildings, roads, fences, utilities, drainage, trees and survey blocks from EA’s CAD.
  Residential design (EA 2025) — limits of excavation, daylight and transition lines, grading breaklines, repository, haul and staging, ESC, alignments, annotation, CAD contours, the sheet overlays, and EA’s design surfaces.
  Investigations — sample results, monitoring wells, borings, imported datasets.
  Cultural resources — protected, off by default, see below.
  My work — your drawings, measurements, sections, design pads and imports, each switchable as a class without losing the per-feature choices underneath.
Each group header has a master checkbox and a count; each row has an opacity slider where it makes sense. The manage… button on the design group opens the Layer manager: search EA’s 110 CAD layer names, switch one on or off, recolour it, set its opacity, and read its source file, feature count and a real entity handle you can type into AutoCAD. Reset to defaults puts everything back.

Limits of excavation — the geodatabase polygons are the authority: they reproduce EA’s printed areas to 0.02%. They are styled by depth — ■ 1.0 ft solid, ■ 0.5 ft dashed — with the depth labelled at the centroid. EA’s CAD draws the same limits as open drafting segments; that group is off by default so the two do not fight over the same click. The four 6 in call-outs EA wrote as leader notes are marked in orange and say plainly that the region was never delineated — verify them before they are dug.

Design surfaces — four surfaces recovered from EA’s own Civil 3D files: EA existing ground and the borrow-area existing ground (recovered byte-for-byte and validated against the lidar at 0.00 and +0.12 ft mean), and the depth-based residential excavation bottom and finished grade. Each carries a kind and a confidence badge and can be used as a volume base, sectioned, draped, and compared. The repository and north-lobe final grades are listed greyed: no proposed-grade TIN exists in the delivered drawings, and the remedy is to ask EA for a LandXML export rather than to invent one.
Isopach draws the cut/fill thickness of any surface against the January-2024 lidar ground — blue where the design stands above the ground, red where it is below, legend in feet, in 2D and draped in 3D. Volume in polygon vs surface measures any polygon against any surface, and an excavation polygon offers volume of this excavation, which shows area × depth and the raster method side by side so you can see the two agree.

Cultural resources — recorded archaeological locations from the T22-0762 survey of the Elem Indian Colony, included by an explicit decision of the project lead. Protected under NHPA §304 and ARPA §9. The group is off at every start; the first time you switch it on, an acknowledgement explains the protection. While any of it is visible a red stamp sits over the map and the 3D view and is burned into every image the app exports, and an export that would carry the geometry asks again and writes the notice into the file.

Terrain — the January 2024 survey’s lidar-derived 1-ft DEM (LandXML gridded surface, 42.6M points), regridded at 2 ft site-wide and kept at 1 ft over the mine area. Coordinates are NAD83(2011) California State Plane Zone 2, US survey feet; elevations in feet. Imagery is the same flight at three resolutions, finest on top.

Drafting — object snap (F3) locks onto endpoints, midpoints, intersections, the perpendicular foot and the nearest point on a segment, across your drawings, the DU and pile outlines, the contours, EA’s native geometry and the sample points. Hold Shift for ortho; POLAR (F10) tracks 15°. While sketching, just start typing: 150 is a distance along the current direction, @150,75 a relative offset, @150<45 relative polar, and 6371500,2128900 an absolute State Plane point.
The command line takes the aliases you already know — PL POLY VOL PR ID DI OFFSET MIRROR ROTATE COPY MOVE JOIN EXPLODE DIM TEXT PAD BAL SEC WAND CBOUND TOE STANDS TREES DXFOUT DXFIN SAVE OPEN ZE ZW 3D TABLE SHEETS DATASET ISOPACH VOLSURF LAYERS — with autocomplete, ↑ history, and HELP for the full sheet.

Smart boundaries — one click instead of tracing a footprint. WAND delineates a waste pile by morphological top-hat, which is the ABP memo’s own method; CBOUND traces the closed terrain contour through a point; TOE traces where slope crosses a threshold; STANDS polygonises canopy over a height threshold for clearing limits. Each produces an ordinary editable feature.

Sheet viewer — all 20 plan sheets exactly as plotted, at about 117 dpi, from the Sheets tab, a sheet’s footprint on the map, its draped sheet in 3D, or the SHEETS command. Inside a window: scroll to zoom toward the cursor, drag to pan, fit / 1:1, ‹ › to flip through the set, locate to fly the map to that sheet. A registered sheet also lets you measure and mark on the paper: marks become ordinary features in State Plane coordinates that appear in 2D and 3D immediately. Twelve sheets are georeferenced; C-101, C-102 and C-203 are not, and say so — their design geometry is in the app anyway, from EA’s native files. C-202 draws the North Lobe twice (grading plan and planting plan): a mark is placed through whichever plan it is made on, and the title block and notes are refused.

Volume = neat in-place topographic quantity against a base surface (perimeter TIN reproduces the ABP memo Attachment E method; also best-fit plane, lowest rim, fixed elevation, or any design surface). Planning-level — report to 2 significant figures. Volume integration, the terrain-analysis rasters, the isopach and computed contours all run in background workers, so the interface stays responsive and every job can be cancelled.

Water — two static terrain analyses over the same lidar bare earth. Raindrop (R, DROP) drops a marker where you click and runs it downhill by steepest descent, on the finest DEM that covers it. Where the run reaches a low point it ponds: the depression is filled to its pour point and drawn with its level, depth, area and volume, and the drop carries on from there — a pond deeper than 0.25 ft is real terrain, anything shallower is lidar noise and is crossed silently. The run ends at the edge of the surveyed ground (Clear Lake or the survey limit), in a depression with no outlet inside the search window, or at the length cap; if it leaves its window it is re-run on whichever grid covers where it left, and the card lists the grids it used. The result is an ordinary feature: drag the raindrop to retrace it, and ask it for an elevation profile or its contributing area.
Overtopping (OVERTOP, the Water menu, or a water polygon’s popup) takes the water surface the lidar actually recorded and raises it until it spills. It reports the spill elevation, where it spills, the freeboard, the storage and area it takes to get there, and the route the overflow then takes downhill. Around the water it paints a ring of rim elevations — hot where the rim is at the spill, fading out as it rises — with the low points ranked ①②③, and a slider walks the level from today’s surface past the spill.
Neither tool is hydraulics: there is no rainfall, runoff, infiltration, seepage, wave run-up or time in any of it. They say what the ground shape implies, at planning level.

Export — GeoJSON in WGS84 or State Plane (EPSG:6418), DXF R12 in raw State Plane feet for AutoCAD and Civil 3D, session files you can reload or share, and CSVs of results, samples and datasets. Drag-drop a GeoJSON, DXF or CSV onto the map to import it.

In the field — (also the FIELD command) lays the whole app out for a phone: a bottom bar with Position, Inspect, Raindrop, Photo, Note and Layers, the docks as sheets that slide up from the bottom, popups as full-width cards, and 44-px targets throughout. It switches itself on at start on a touch device with a narrow screen, and your choice is remembered.
Position uses the browser's own geolocation and converts it through the site affine (±1 ft) — nothing is ever placed without a real fix. Photo takes a picture, reads its EXIF orientation, time and GPS, and puts it on the map at its own tag, else at the device position, else where you tap; it becomes an ordinary feature that appears in My work, exports, drapes in 3D and travels in the session file (the GeoJSON export asks before it carries the images). Note is a text feature through a big-button flow, and Samples nearby lists the twenty nearest sample locations to where you are standing.
On a tablet — the app detects a touch device and turns on 44-px targets, a visible ⋯ button on every layer row, long-press for what a right-click does, an on-screen nav pad in 3D, and pinch / twist / three-finger gestures in the 3D view and the sheet viewer; a sketch gets a magnifying loupe and a Done / Undo vertex / Cancel bar, because a finger covers the point it is placing and there is no Enter key.
Add to Home Screen — in Safari, tap the Share button, scroll to “Add to Home Screen”, then Add. It then opens full screen with its own settings, like an app.
 Offline — offline copy: none
Apple Pencil — a pen tap places a vertex exactly where the tip is (no loupe, mouse-size snapping), a pen hover moves the osnap glyph and the 3D highlight, and your palm is ignored while the tip is down. REDLINE (also MARKUP, INK) is freehand ink on the map and inside a sheet window: pressure drives the width, six colours and an eraser, one undo per stroke, and every stroke is a normal feature — it exports to GeoJSON and to DXF on a REDLINE layer, drapes in 3D and prints in the report. In 3D, a pen drag orbits and a pen drag with a finger held down pans.
